CS 255 – Systems Analysis and Design
DriverPass Project

This repository contains my work from CS 255 at Southern New Hampshire University. The two projects focus on gathering business requirements and designing a system based on a client's needs.

Project Summary

The client for this project was DriverPass, a company that wanted to help students prepare for their driving tests. They needed an online system where customers could schedule driving lessons, take practice tests, access training materials, and track their progress. The system also needed to help employees manage appointments, customer information, and reports while keeping everything secure.

What did you do particularly well?

I think one thing I did well was breaking down the client's requirements and organizing them into something easier to understand. The interview transcript had a lot of information, and I had to figure out which details were important for the system. I also think I did a good job creating the UML diagrams, especially after getting more comfortable with Lucidchart. They helped me see how the different parts of the system would work together.

What would you revise or improve?

If I could go back and improve one part of my work, I would focus on the UML diagrams, especially the connections between different components. Some diagrams became complicated because of how many relationships and interactions needed to be represented. I would spend more time planning the layout before creating them so they would be easier to read and understand.

How did you interpret the user's needs and implement them into your system design?

I started by reviewing the DriverPass interview and identifying what the client wanted the system to accomplish. From there, I separated the requirements into functional and nonfunctional requirements. For example, customers needed to schedule driving lessons online, while employees needed different levels of access depending on their responsibilities. I used these requirements to guide my diagrams and overall system design. Considering the user's needs is important because even a system that works perfectly from a technical standpoint isn't useful if it doesn't solve the problems it was designed for.

How do you approach designing software?

My approach is to first understand the problem before trying to build a solution. I like breaking larger problems into smaller pieces because it makes them easier to manage. In the future, I would continue using tools like UML diagrams, requirement documents, and flowcharts to organize my ideas before writing code. I also think getting feedback throughout the design process is important because it can help catch problems early instead of having to fix them later.
